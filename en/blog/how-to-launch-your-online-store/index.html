How to launch your online store (and make it actually sell): the no-nonsense guide

Jumping Panda Team
Chio, Erci and Roberto

Opening an online store has never been easier. In one afternoon you can have something published and show it off on social media. But here is the part almost nobody tells you: opening the store is the easy part. Getting it to sell is another story.

We have seen plenty of beautiful stores with zero sales, and plain ones that never stop selling. The difference is almost never the design. It is what was decided before opening. Let's go step by step 👇

Before you open: what problem do you solve?

This is THE question. Not "what do I sell", but which problem do I solve for whom. Everything else comes from there: who you talk to, what makes you different and why they would buy from you and not from the store next door.

Take some time to answer three things:

  • Who do you sell to? The more specific, the better. "Everyone" is the same as "no one".
  • What makes you different? Take a tour of your competitors' stores. What do they do well? What do they leave half-done that you can do better?
  • What numbers do you need for this to be worth it? You do not need a 50-page business plan. If you know your costs, your margin and a realistic sales goal, you are already ahead of most people.

Where will your store live?

The platform matters, but do not get stuck here for too long: to sell products online, options like Shopify already solve the hard parts (cart, payments, shipping, inventory). What we do ask is that the decision be a conscious one and not "the one my cousin uses". We wrote a full guide about that: Which platform should you choose for your website? Read it and come back, we will be here 🐼.

Your product has to look (and read) well

In a physical store, the customer touches the product. Online, your photos and your copy do that job:

  • Quality photos, several of them, in context. You do not need a professional studio, but you do need good light, clean backgrounds and photos of the product in use. It is one of the investments that shows up most in sales.
  • Descriptions that tell a story. Do not list features: explain what the customer gains. "100% cotton T-shirt" informs; "the T-shirt that survives the wash without losing its shape" sells. And using the words your customer searches with also helps Google find you.
  • Inventory up to date. Selling something you no longer have is the fastest way to burn a new customer's trust. Turn on low-stock alerts from day one.

Shipping: decide this before the first sale

Nothing is more stressful than getting your first order and not knowing how you will send it. Decide ahead of time which carriers you use (USPS, UPS, FedEx or others), how much you charge for shipping (or from what amount it is free) and how long delivery takes. Promise times you can actually meet. A shipment that arrives when you said it would is worth more than a "fast" one that arrives late.

No trust, no sale

Think like a buyer: would you give your card to a store that looks sketchy? Neither will your customer. Three basics:

  • Familiar payment options. Offer options your customer recognizes: credit and debit cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, PayPal, and where it applies, buy-now-pay-later. The more familiar the checkout, the fewer abandoned carts.
  • The security padlock (SSL). It is the certificate that protects your customers' data and makes the browser show your site as secure. Serious platforms already include it, just verify it is active.
  • Clear policies. Returns, shipping, privacy. They do not have to be perfect on day one, but they do have to exist and be easy to find. A store without a returns policy scares people off.

People do not show up on their own (yes, again)

We have said it before on this blog and we will keep saying it: launching your store is the beginning, not the end. Without a plan to attract visits, your store is a beautiful shop on a street where nobody walks by. The main paths:

  • SEO: get Google to show you when people search for what you sell. It is traffic you do not pay per click for, but it takes time, which is why you start on day one, not "later".
  • Ads: Google and Meta bring you customers from the first week, if the campaign is well built and the store is ready to convert.
  • Social media: consistent content that shows your product in action and builds community.
  • Email: capture emails from the start. A customer who already bought from you is the easiest one to sell to again. Do not let them go.

You do not have to do all four from day one. But you do need at least one working, on purpose.
